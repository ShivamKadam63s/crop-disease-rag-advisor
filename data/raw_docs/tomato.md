## Early blight
type: disease
aliases: Alternaria leaf spot; target spot; early blight of tomato
pathogen: Alternaria solani (fungus)
symptoms: brown spots with concentric rings like a target board on older lower leaves; yellow patch around the leaf spots and leaves dry up and fall; dark sunken spots on the stem near the soil; dark leathery spots near the stalk end of the fruit; disease starts on lower leaves and moves upward
favorable_conditions: warm weather with alternating dry and humid spells, dew, plant stress and poor nutrition
cultural_control: Remove and destroy infected leaves and crop debris. Rotate with non-solanaceous crops. Avoid overhead irrigation and keep plants spaced and staked for airflow.
biological_control: Seed or soil treatment with Trichoderma based products can reduce early infection.
chemical_control: Protective fungicides such as mancozeb, chlorothalonil or copper-based products are commonly used. Follow the product label for dose and spray interval.

## Late blight
type: disease
aliases: Phytophthora blight; late blight of tomato
pathogen: Phytophthora infestans (oomycete)
symptoms: large water soaked greasy dark patches on leaves that turn brown quickly; white downy fungal growth on the underside of leaves in humid mornings; dark brown firm blotches on green fruit; black lesions on stems and the whole plant collapses within days; spreads very fast in cool cloudy wet weather
favorable_conditions: cool temperatures, high humidity, cloudy and rainy weather with long leaf wetness
cultural_control: Destroy infected plants and volunteer plants. Avoid leaf wetness by drip irrigation and wide spacing. Use tolerant varieties where available.
biological_control: No dependable biological control; rely on prevention and sanitation.
chemical_control: Protective sprays such as mancozeb or copper oxychloride are used before infection, and systemic mixtures containing metalaxyl may be used as per label. Follow the product label for dose and spray interval.

## Tomato leaf curl virus
type: disease
aliases: ToLCV; leaf curl; tomato leaf curl disease; begomovirus
pathogen: Tomato leaf curl begomovirus spread by whitefly
symptoms: leaves curl upward and become small and crinkled; veins look thick and leaves feel leathery; plants stay stunted and bushy; flowers drop and very few fruits set; whiteflies are seen on the underside of leaves
favorable_conditions: warm dry weather with high whitefly population, early infection in nursery
cultural_control: Raise seedlings under insect proof net. Uproot and destroy infected plants early. Use yellow sticky traps and remove weeds that host whitefly. There is no cure for infected plants.
biological_control: Neem based sprays and other bio-pesticides can reduce whitefly numbers.
chemical_control: Insecticides such as imidacloprid may be used against whitefly vectors as per label. Follow the product label for dose and spray interval.

## Bacterial wilt
type: disease
aliases: Ralstonia wilt; southern wilt; bacterial wilt of tomato
pathogen: Ralstonia solanacearum (bacterium)
symptoms: sudden wilting of green plants in the daytime without yellowing; plants may look fine at night at first but later wilt permanently; brown discoloration inside the cut stem; cut stem placed in clear water gives milky white streaming ooze; wilting begins in a few plants and spreads along the field
favorable_conditions: warm wet soil, poorly drained fields and infested soil
cultural_control: Rotate with cereals or other non-solanaceous crops. Use well drained raised beds and resistant varieties or rootstocks. Remove wilted plants with the root soil. There is no effective curative spray.
biological_control: Soil amendments with organic matter and antagonistic bacteria can help in reducing the disease pressure.
chemical_control: No chemical treatment reliably cures bacterial wilt. Follow local agriculture officer advice and the product label.
